THE ONE-LINE LAUGH — candidate field
(notes: strength / weakness, judged on late turn, everyday truth, economy)

1. I bought a reusable water bottle to save the planet and now I own eleven of them.
   + Recognition is instant, "eleven" is a good specific.
   - The turn ("own eleven") is a listing joke, not a recast; six words of setup are
     spent on the moral ("to save the planet") that the punch then repeats.

2. I cleaned the flat before the cleaner arrived, because I didn't want her to think I needed a cleaner.
   + Best logical loop in the field: the act of hiring proves the thing you're hiding.
   - "Clean before the cleaner" is a well-worn premise; a judge reading 40 entries has
     probably seen this skeleton twice already. Originality risk outweighs the laugh.

3. I read a whole book about decluttering, and the only thing I can't throw away is the book.
   + Tight paradox, tidy last two words, no wasted clause.
   - Also a familiar gag shape ("the decluttering book is the clutter"); reads as clever
     rather than as a scene you have lived through.

4. I always bring a book to parties, which is a polite way of bringing a door.
   + Genuinely surprising recast — "door" retro-explains the book.
   - Only lands for the introvert half of the room; needs a metaphor to work, and
     metaphor is the opposite of an everyday truth stated plainly.

5. I opened my phone to check the weather and emerged forty minutes later knowing the weather.
   + Very true, very economical, flat anticlimax is a real technique.
   - The surprise is small: nothing is recast, the sentence just confirms what the
     reader already suspected by word eight.

6. I sorted my books by colour, which is the prettiest way I've ever made them impossible to find.
   + Aesthetic vs utility reversal is a real laugh, and the last word carries it.
   - "Sorting books by colour" is a design-blog habit, not a universal one; half the
     readers have never done it and the recognition goes soft.

7. My mother asked if I was eating properly, and I listed a meal I'd cooked in March.
   + Warm, universal, "in March" is a hard little punch; the lie is affectionate.
   - Joke depends on the reader having moved out and having a mother who asks; the
     turn is a specific date rather than a reversal, so it amuses more than surprises.

8. I turned on two-factor authentication for safety, and now the only person who can't get into my account is me.
   + Self-defeating loop, "is me" lands late, everybody has done this.
   - The word "authentication" spends the reader's attention before the joke starts;
     administrative vocabulary flattens a one-liner.

9. I left my gym bag by the front door to remove all excuses; it is now the fittest thing in my flat.
   + Strong late turn (the bag out-trains the owner), one clean image.
   - The punch is a conceit — "fittest" only works if you accept the bag as an athlete,
     so the reader laughs at the wording, not at themselves.

10. I left my gym clothes by the door as motivation, and now I step over them on the way to the fridge.
   + Three beats in one breath: good intention, flat "and now", and a destination that
     is the punch. "The fridge" is the last thing you read and it retro-explains the
     whole sentence: the clothes aren't ignored, they're a route marker on the only
     journey this person actually makes. Recognition is physical — the reader has
     stepped over that exact pile.
   - Slightly gentler than a pun; the laugh is a wince of recognition rather than a
     snap of wordplay. Accepted: the brief asks for an everyday truth, not a pun.

WINNER: 10.
Runner-up: 2 (better loop, disqualified for familiarity).
